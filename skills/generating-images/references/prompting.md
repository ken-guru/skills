# Writing image prompts

## Start from what the image must do

Before writing the prompt, state the image's job in one sentence: "Make the audience feel the scale of the outage" or "Show who the product is for". Every detail in the prompt should serve that job.

## Prompt structure

Write the prompt as a short paragraph in this order:

1. **Subject**: who or what, doing what.
2. **Setting**: where and when.
3. **Composition**: framing and where the subject sits, leaving space where text will go next to it (for example "subject on the right third, calm empty space on the left").
4. **Style**: photographic, illustrated, or flat; colour mood; lighting.
5. **Theme hint**, when there is one: match the deck or document's colours and tone ("warm paper tones, muted coral accents").

Example:

> A small team gathered around one laptop in a bright, plain meeting room, mid-discussion and smiling. Medium shot, people on the right half of the frame, a clean wall on the left. Natural editorial photography, soft daylight, muted warm colours.

## Avoid

- **Text in the image.** The script adds a rule against it; do not ask for signs, labels, screens with words, or charts.
- **Real, identifiable people** (public figures, colleagues) and trademarked logos or characters.
- **Implying something false.** A generated image must not pass as a photo of a real event, product, or person. The visible "AI-generated illustration" label is required even when the image looks like a photograph.

## Shape

Use `--shape landscape` for full-width placements, `portrait` for a side panel, and `square` for small inline images.
