# Storyline

## Shape

1. **Key message**: one sentence the audience should be able to repeat afterwards.
2. **Arc**: the order that moves this audience from where they are to the goal. Common arcs:
   - *Situation → complication → resolution*: for proposals and decisions.
   - *Problem → why it matters → what to do*: for persuasion.
   - *Before → what changed → after*: for updates and results.
   - *Question → evidence → answer*: for research findings.
3. **Sections**: two to five, each a step in the arc.
4. **Slide topics**: one line each, stating the slide's point as a claim, with the sources it rests on.

## Format

```markdown
---
approved: false
---

# Storyline: Why boring technology wins

Key message: Spend your few chances to adopt new technology on what makes your product different.
Arc: problem → why it matters → what to do. Length: 5 minutes, about 6 slides.

## 1. The pull of the new
- Slide: New tools are tempting, and every team adopts some each year. (opinion)
- Slide: Each new tool spends an innovation token; a team has only a few. [S1]

## 2. What it costs
- Slide: Unfamiliar tools fail in unfamiliar ways. [S2]

## 3. What to do instead
- Slide: Spend tokens on your product's edge, not on plumbing. [S1]
- Slide: Before adopting a tool, ask three questions. (speaker's own experience)
```

## Rules

- Every factual claim cites the Source Set; opinions and the speaker's own experience are labelled.
- Fit the length in the Brief: about one slide per minute for a typical talk, leaving the agreed time for questions.
- When the person asks to move, drop, or add sections, apply every change and say plainly when a requested change has nothing to act on.
