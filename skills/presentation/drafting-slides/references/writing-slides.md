# Writing slides

## Headings state the point

A heading is the one thing the audience should take from the slide, as a short sentence: "Requests doubled this year", not "Traffic". Someone reading only the headings should follow the whole argument.

## Bodies support the heading

- One idea per slide. When a slide needs two points, split it (the two slides may share a heading).
- At most about five bullets of a few words each, or one short paragraph, or one small table.
- Numbers on slides are rounded and have units. The exact figure goes in the notes with its citation.
- Never put on a slide what the speaker will read word for word: the slide shows, the speaker tells.

## Speaker notes are the script

- Write them as spoken sentences the speaker can read aloud, in their voice.
- Start with the sentence that introduces the slide; end with the bridge to the next.
- Every factual claim carries its citation, for example `[S2]`. Opinions and personal experience are labelled as such.
- Put long descriptions of diagrams and the data tables of charts here, so people who cannot see the visual can still follow.

## Timing

Plan about one slide per minute for a typical talk, fewer for a talk with demos or discussion. Check the total against the Brief's length, leaving the agreed time for questions, and say when the draft is likely to run over.

## Language and tone

Use the Brief's language and its editorial preferences (tone, prefer, avoid). Keep technical terms, names, numbers, and citations exactly as the sources give them.
